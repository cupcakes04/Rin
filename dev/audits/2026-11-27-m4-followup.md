## 1. Critical: Step 6b's rejection of starvation (`468c` at `N=25`) is a false signal — D-40's schedule will fail on a non-COCO class in M5

- **Where:** `dev/research/m4_first_curve/README.md:33-57`, `dev/notes/decisions.md:98` (D-40), `dev/audits/2026-11-27.md:167`, and `workspace/store/runs/20261127-183232-yolo.train-468c/files/results.csv`.
- **What actually happened inside `468c` (`N=25`, M4's `accumulate=8`, 25 steps):**

  | Run | `N` | `acc` | `best_ep` | `ep1 val` | `ep10 val` | `ep50 val` | `ep50 box / cls loss` | Test `mAP50-95` |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `468c` | 25 | 8 | **1** | **0.560** | **0.060** | **0.319** | **1.698 / 1.404** | **0.634** |
  | `57a5` | 25 | 1 | 50 | 0.247 | 0.0001 | **0.578** | 0.937 / 0.510 | 0.557 |
  | `9e7c` | 25 (`k=250`) | 1 | 37 | 0.529 | 0.512 | 0.577 | 0.347 / 0.143 | 0.531 |
  | `f9f0` | 100 | 8 | 48 | 0.440 | 0.504 | 0.642 | 0.893 / 0.490 | 0.620 |
  | `369f` | 100 | 1 | 45 | 0.482 | 0.504 | 0.639 | 0.822 / 0.416 | 0.608 |

- **Look at `468c`:**
  1. At `N=25, accumulate=8`, `468c` **never converged** — its `val mAP` crashed from `0.560` (epoch 1) to `0.060` (epoch 10) and only crawled back to `0.319` by epoch 50 (`box_loss = 1.698`, `cls_loss = 1.404`; epochs 2–3 and 4–5 didn't even take an optimizer step, producing bit-identical val scores).
  2. The only reason `468c` scored `0.634` mAP50-95 (`0.889` mAP50, `0.00` empty-frame FPs — virtually identical to stock `0.629 / 0.885 / 0.00`) is that **`best.pt` was saved at epoch 1**, before training had moved the weights away from stock COCO's class-0 `person` detector.
  3. Meanwhile, `57a5` (`acc=1`, 200 steps) actually recovered and trained (`val 0.578` at epoch 50 vs `468c`'s `0.319`), and lost test mAP because (a) it was trained on 25 noisy unchecked frames with `lr0 = 0.002` **unscaled** for an 8× smaller effective batch size (`batch 8` vs `64`), and (b) `468c` reverted to stock COCO at epoch 1.
- **Why this matters critically for the new M5 (D-17):** On a **rare concept COCO lacks**, stock epoch 1 scores **`0.000`** — there is no COCO safety net to fall back on. If M5 runs `N=25` (or `N=50`) with D-40's `accumulate=8, epochs=50` (~25–44 AdamW steps), small budgets will genuinely collapse/starve (like `468c`'s epoch 50) while `N=500` gets ~400 steps.
- **Action before M5's sweep:** Once the new non-COCO concept is chosen, re-test convergence (`best_ep`, `train/cls_loss`, `val mAP`) at the smallest budget on *that* concept, and if changing `accumulate`, scale `lr0` accordingly (or scale `epochs` at fixed `accumulate` / `nbs`) rather than carrying `468c`'s `person` conclusion into a from-scratch class.

## 2. Design trap for M5 in `curate.select`: `stream_sql()` groups by `(camera, day)`

- **Where:** `src/rin/curate/select.py:87-121`, `src/rin/curate/streams.py:19-22`, `dev/notes/decisions.md:94` (D-38).
- **What happens:** `curate.select` groups candidates using `stream_sql()`, which keys on `(site, camera, split, DATE(started_at))`. In M4 the pool was one day (`2018-03-07`), so stream == camera.
- In M5 ("long fixed-camera footage" across multiple days/cameras/hours), a pool of e.g. 2 cameras × 5 days becomes **10 separate streams**:
  1. `_in_turns` forces an equal budget per `(camera, day)` — giving a day where the rare concept never appears the same share as a day where it does.
  2. `kcenter` runs independently per `(camera, day)` (cannot cover across days of the same camera), and `kmeans_stratified` requires `clusters <= len(members)` on *every single* `(camera, day)` stream.
- **Fix when M5 goes multi-day:** Keep `(camera, day)` for `dedupe`/`center`, but group `select` by `(site, camera, split)` (or make grouping explicit) so multi-day footage of one camera is selected as one camera stream.

## 3. Small bugs in the new code

1. **`dev/scripts/superseded_runs.py` (`--delete`) can fail mid-run on same-second runs:**
   - **Where:** `dev/scripts/superseded_runs.py:46-58`.
   - Sorting `gone` by `run_id` descending assumes newer runs sort after older runs. When two runs start in the **same second** (which already happened repeatedly in M4, e.g. `20261127-152506-yolo.predict-*` and `20261127-152506-evaluate.coco-*`), `evaluate.coco` sorts alphabetically *before* `yolo.predict` (`e < y`), so reverse-sorted deletion tries to delete the parent (`yolo.predict`) before its child (`evaluate.coco`) and crashes inside `m.delete`. Use a topological sort over `inputs` instead of string reverse sort.
   - *(Minor nit on line 24: `\b[0-9a-f]{4}\b` also matches 4-digit numbers like `1000`, `1280`, `2026`, or hex words like `fabe` in prose, harmlessly over-keeping matching runs.)*
2. **`_clamped()` in `yolo/__init__.py` silently drops `NaN` box coordinates instead of raising:**
   - **Where:** `src/rin/servants/yolo/__init__.py:138-142,225`.
   - Line 140 uses `any(v < -BOX_SLACK or v > 1 + BOX_SLACK for v in box)`. For `float('nan')`, both comparisons are `False`, `min(max(nan, 0.0), 1.0)` stays `nan`, and line 225 (`box[0] < box[2]`) evaluates `False`, silently dropping a `NaN` box into `boxes_dropped_no_area` instead of failing loudly. Change line 140 to `any(not (-BOX_SLACK <= v <= 1 + BOX_SLACK) for v in box)`.

## 4. M5 rare-concept risks worth keeping in mind while planning

- **Whole-frame DINOv3 vs a small rare concept:** M4 step 6c showed whole-frame DINOv3 `kcenter` and `kmeans_stratified` found the quiet gym (`dev/research/m4_selection/README.md:42`) because 50 people leaving a room changes the entire frame. If M5's rare concept is a *small/local* object on a fixed camera (e.g., an object carried by a person, a small prop, a vest/helmet), whole-frame DINOv3 CLS may miss it while `EmbeddingGemma 2` text search or patch-difference features (`dev/notes/ideas/holy_grail.md` §4) won't. Keep an eye on that during M5's concept screening.
- **Test-set design for a rare concept (`dev/notes/ideas/where_distillation_pays.md:26`):** Avoid using SAM3 or DINOv3 to choose which frames enter the test set (circularity / D-30). Selecting test *clips/hours* where the concept occurs and sampling uniformly/randomly inside them (keeping plenty of negatives so empty-frame FPs stay measurable) avoids model bias while keeping positives dense enough to evaluate AP.
